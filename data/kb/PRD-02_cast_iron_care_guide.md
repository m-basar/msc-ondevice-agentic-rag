# Cast Iron Care Guide

## Seasoning

Seasoning is a layer of polymerised oil bonded to the iron. It gives the non-stick surface and protects against rust. It is built up, not applied once.

To season: wash and dry thoroughly, apply a very thin coat of a high smoke point oil such as rapeseed or grapeseed, wipe out until the surface looks dry, then bake upside down at 220°C for one hour with a tray beneath. Let it cool in the oven. Repeat two or three times for a new or stripped pan.

Too much oil is the most common mistake. It leaves a sticky, patchy surface rather than a hard one.

## Everyday washing

Wash by hand with hot water and a stiff brush while the pan is still warm. A small amount of washing-up liquid is fine on a well-seasoned pan; the modern detergents that would strip seasoning are no longer in common use. Never put cast iron in a dishwasher.

Dry immediately and completely, on a low hob ring if necessary, then wipe a trace of oil over the cooking surface.

## Stuck-on food

Simmer a little water in the pan for a few minutes and scrape with a wooden spatula, or scour with coarse salt and a paper towel. Do not soak.

## Rust

Rust is not a fault and is not covered by warranty. Scour the rust off with steel wool, wash, dry, and re-season from bare metal.

## What not to cook

Highly acidic foods such as tomato sauce and wine reductions will strip a young seasoning if simmered for a long time. A well-established seasoning tolerates them.

## Enamelled cast iron

Enamelled pans are not seasoned and must not be. Avoid thermal shock: never put a hot enamelled pan into cold water, as the enamel will craze.
