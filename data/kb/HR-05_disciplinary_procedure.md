# Disciplinary Procedure

## Principles

This procedure follows the ACAS Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures. Its purpose is to encourage improvement rather than to punish. No employee will be dismissed for a first offence except in cases of gross misconduct.

## Informal stage

Minor issues are addressed informally by the line manager through a documented conversation. A note is placed on file but this is not a formal warning.

## Formal stages

| Stage | Outcome | Live for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First written warning | 6 months |
| 2 | Final written warning | 12 months |
| 3 | Dismissal or other sanction | - |

## Hearings

The employee receives written notice of the allegations, copies of any evidence, and at least five working days to prepare. The employee has a statutory right to be accompanied by a work colleague or trade union representative.

Hearings are chaired by a manager not previously involved in the matter. A note-taker attends. The outcome is confirmed in writing within five working days.

## Gross misconduct

Conduct that may constitute gross misconduct includes theft, fraud, physical violence, serious breach of health and safety rules, operating equipment under the influence of alcohol or drugs, deliberate damage to company property, and serious breaches of the data protection policy.

Where gross misconduct is alleged, the employee may be suspended on full pay while the matter is investigated. Suspension is a neutral act and is not a disciplinary sanction.

## Appeals

An employee may appeal in writing within five working days of receiving the outcome. Appeals are heard by a more senior manager who has had no prior involvement.
