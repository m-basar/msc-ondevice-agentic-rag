# Data Retention Schedule

## Principle

Personal data is kept only as long as necessary for the purpose it was collected for, or as long as the law requires. Keeping data indefinitely is a breach of the storage limitation principle in the UK GDPR.

## Schedule

| Record type | Retention period | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Employee personnel file | 6 years | After employment ends |
| Recruitment records, unsuccessful applicants | 6 months | After the decision |
| Payroll records | 6 years | After the tax year |
| Working time records | 2 years | From the record date |
| Accident book entries | 3 years | After the entry, or after the person turns 21 if a minor |
| RIDDOR reports | 3 years | After the report |
| Health surveillance records | 40 years | After the record |
| Customer order and invoice records | 7 years | After the end of the financial year |
| Statutory accounts and tax records | 7 years | After the financial year |
| Supplier contracts | 7 years | After expiry |
| CCTV footage | 31 days | From recording |
| Website analytics and marketing consent | 2 years | From last interaction |
| Security incident log | 6 years | After closure |
| Job applicant right to work checks | 2 years | After employment ends |

## Reporting a suspected breach

Suspected breaches of this schedule, including data retained past its period or disposed of early, are reported to the Data Protection Lead within 72 hours.

## Disposal

At the end of the retention period, electronic records are deleted, including from backups at the next backup cycle rollover, and paper records are cross-cut shredded. Disposal of confidential waste is carried out under a certificate of destruction.

Deletion is not performed where a record is subject to an ongoing legal claim, investigation or regulatory request. In that case the record is placed on legal hold and the reason recorded.

## Review

The Data Protection Lead reviews this schedule annually against current legislation.
