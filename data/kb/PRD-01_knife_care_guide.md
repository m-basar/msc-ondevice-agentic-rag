# Knife Care Guide

## Washing

Wash knives by hand in warm soapy water and dry immediately. Dishwashers damage knives in three ways: the detergent is abrasive and dulls the edge, the heat can loosen or crack handle scales, and blades knocking against other items chip the edge. Dishwasher damage is not covered by the warranty described in CS-03.

Never leave a knife soaking in a sink. It damages the handle and it is a serious cutting hazard for the next person.

## Cutting surfaces

Use wood or plastic boards. Glass, marble, stone and ceramic boards will blunt an edge within a few cuts and can chip it. Do not cut on a plate, and do not use the blade to scrape the board, as this rolls the edge; turn the knife over and use the spine.

## Sharpening and honing

Honing with a steel realigns an edge that has rolled and should be done every few uses. It does not remove metal and does not sharpen.

Sharpening removes metal and restores the edge. A whetstone gives the best result. For most European knives, sharpen at 20 degrees per side; for Japanese knives, 15 degrees per side. Pull-through sharpeners are convenient but remove more metal than necessary and shorten the life of a good knife.

Sharpen when the knife no longer slices a tomato skin cleanly under its own weight.

## Storage

Store in a knife block, on a magnetic strip, or in an edge guard. Loose in a drawer, blades knock together and chip.

## Stainless and carbon steel

Stainless resists rust but is not rustproof; dry it. Carbon steel takes a keener edge and holds it longer, but will rust within hours if left wet, and will develop a dark patina with use. The patina is normal and protective. Rust is not covered by the warranty.
